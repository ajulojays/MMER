# MMER / CowPAL hypothesis and replication framework

Updated: 2026-08-14

## Scientific objective

MMER tests whether the ecological state of the mammary microbiome before perturbation contains information about its subsequent ecological perturbability.

The general biological hypothesis is:

`Perturbability = f(Baseline ecological state, perturbation context, background)`

rather than perturbability being determined solely by treatment exposure.

This document freezes the distinction between discovery, replication, sensitivity analysis, and refined hypotheses generated by discordant cross-study results.

---

## Study roles

### Study 1 — discovery cohort

Study 1 is Biscarini et al. 2020 / PRJEB38332.

The biological unit is a mammary-quarter trajectory. Five cows each contribute four quarter trajectories, giving 20 trajectories and 60 total samples.

Timepoints:

- T1: dry-off, pre-intervention baseline
- T2: calving
- T3: 5 days in milk

Study 1 generated the ecological susceptibility hypothesis. The portable baseline predictor set was restricted to:

- richness
- Shannon diversity
- evenness
- dominance

The Study 1 directional signature was:

- richness: positive association with perturbability
- Shannon: positive
- evenness: positive
- dominance: negative

The trajectory-level target was:

`mean Bray perturbability = mean[d(T1,T2), d(T1,T3)]`

Nested leave-one-cow-out validation of the ecology-only model yielded RMSE 0.10655, Pearson r 0.557, Spearman rho 0.589, and CV R² 0.294. Treatment alone performed substantially worse, and adding treatment to baseline ecology did not improve generalization.

Study 1 is considered discovery-complete. Because it contains only five cows and treatment is confounded with anatomical quarter, it is not treated as proof of a universal ecological rule.

### Study 2 — independent replication/generalization cohort

Study 2 is Van Beeck et al. / PRJEB63336.

The primary MMER replication cohort comprises milk trajectories across three dairies and four treatment/control strata.

Timepoints:

- Baseline: dry-off, pre-treatment
- 7 Days: 7 days after dry-off/treatment
- 55–75 DIM: 55–75 days in milk in the subsequent lactation

Important temporal-design difference: Study 1 T2 is calving, whereas Study 2 T2 is 7 days after dry-off. These intervals are not biologically equivalent and must not be described as direct temporal replicas.

The source study selected a high-SCC quarter for microbiome sampling, but the currently reconstructed deposited metadata do not explicitly encode anatomical quarter identity across all three longitudinal samples. Until this is verified directly, MMER should avoid stating that the exact same anatomical quarter was maintained at every Study 2 timepoint.

---

## Primary Study 2 cohort

The raw complete milk trajectory set contains 67 cows × 3 timepoints = 201 biological samples.

After bacterial-only filtering, the primary sequencing-depth threshold was fixed at 3,000 bacterial reads per sample because it retained 60 of 67 complete trajectories (89.6%) while avoiding the very shallow libraries.

Primary 3k cohort:

- 60 complete trajectories
- 180 milk samples
- Dairy 1: 14 cows
- Dairy 2: 16 cows
- Dairy 3: 30 cows

Treatment/control counts:

- CB: 19
- CH: 15
- control high SCC: 12
- control low SCC: 14

Dairy × treatment structure:

| Dairy | CB | CH | control high SCC | control low SCC | Total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Dairy 1 | 5 | 3 | 2 | 4 | 14 |
| Dairy 2 | 6 | 4 | 3 | 3 | 16 |
| Dairy 3 | 8 | 8 | 7 | 7 | 30 |
| Total | 19 | 15 | 12 | 14 | 60 |

Predeclared depth sensitivities:

- 2,000 reads/sample: 61 complete trajectories
- 4,000 reads/sample: 54 complete trajectories

A 5,000-read cutoff would retain only 47 complete trajectories and is therefore not the primary threshold.

## Q1 — perturbation magnitude

For Study 2:

`D_B7 = Bray(Baseline, 7 Days)`

`D_BL = Bray(Baseline, 55–75 DIM)`

`D_7L = Bray(7 Days, 55–75 DIM)`

The repeated baseline-displacement model was:

`D ~ Interval + Treatment + Dairy + Interval:Treatment + (1|Cow)`

At ASV level, mean displacements were:

- Baseline → 7 Days: 0.9013
- Baseline → 55–75 DIM: 0.9122
- 7 Days → 55–75 DIM: 0.9173

The repeated model found no evidence that displacement magnitude was explained by:

- interval: P = 0.981
- treatment: P = 0.782
- dairy: P = 0.304
- treatment × interval: P = 0.883

Likelihood-ratio tests were concordant.

Interpretation: substantial longitudinal ecological movement occurred, but treatment did not explain the magnitude or temporal pattern of individual baseline-relative displacement after accounting for dairy.

This does not contradict the original Van Beeck paper's group-level community-composition analysis. MMER asks a different question: how far each longitudinal unit moves from its own baseline.

---

## Q3 — ecological susceptibility hypothesis

### Primary replication target

The Study 2 primary Q3 target preserves the Study 1 structure:

`Y = mean[D(Baseline,7 Days), D(Baseline,55–75 DIM)]`

Primary baseline predictors remain frozen as:

- richness
- Shannon diversity
- evenness
- dominance

Primary adjusted model for each feature:

`Y ~ Ecological feature + Dairy + Treatment`

The primary replication criterion is preservation of the Study 1 directional signature:

- richness: positive
- Shannon: positive
- evenness: positive
- dominance: negative

Two-sided inferential tests are reported and coefficient direction is compared with the Study 1 hypothesis. The analysis must not be converted post hoc into one-sided testing.

### Replication hierarchy

Level I — directional replication: Study 2 reproduces the Study 1 coefficient directions.

Level II — statistical replication: the directional signal is supported after multiple-testing correction.

Level III — predictive generalization: a model trained without one dairy predicts the held-out dairy better than a training-fold null predictor, preferably with positive cross-validated R² and broadly consistent fold performance.

---

## Study 2 primary ASV-level Q3 result

The Study 1 directional rule did not replicate at the primary ASV-level endpoint.

For mean baseline displacement, the Study 2 directions were:

- richness: negative
- Shannon: negative
- evenness: negative
- dominance: positive

These are opposite to the Study 1 directional signature. None survived FDR for the primary ASV mean-displacement target.

Leave-one-dairy-out prediction also failed to support a portable ASV-level alpha-diversity model. For the primary mean-displacement target:

- null RMSE: 0.07009
- treatment RMSE: 0.07041
- alpha-ecology RMSE: 0.07151
- ecology + treatment RMSE: 0.07283

Thus alpha ecology was approximately 2.0% worse than the null for pooled out-of-dairy RMSE, and all pooled CV R² values were negative.

ASV CLR/PCA baseline-composition models also failed to beat the null across dairies.

Conclusion: the strong universal-direction version of the Study 1 Q3 hypothesis is not replicated in Study 2 at the primary ASV-level endpoint.

---

## Secondary taxonomic-resolution sensitivity: genus-level Bray

ASV-level Study 2 Bray values were highly compressed near the upper bound. A secondary, biologically motivated sensitivity analysis therefore aggregated bacterial ASVs to genus/higher-taxonomy bins before calculating Bray displacement.

Genus-level means were:

- Baseline → 7 Days: 0.6804
- Baseline → 55–75 DIM: 0.7297
- mean baseline displacement: 0.7051

Genus-level and ASV-level displacement remained strongly correlated:

- Baseline → 7 Days: Pearson r = 0.811; Spearman rho = 0.744
- Baseline → late: Pearson r = 0.767; Spearman rho = 0.719
- mean baseline displacement: Pearson r = 0.821; Spearman rho = 0.766

Interpretation: ASV resolution amplified fine-scale turnover, but the same underlying ecological movement remained visible at genus level.

### Genus-level Q3 association

At genus resolution, baseline alpha ecology strongly associated with later displacement after adjustment for dairy and treatment, but the direction remained opposite to Study 1.

For genus mean baseline displacement:

| Baseline feature | Direction | P | BH q | Incremental R² |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| richness | negative | 0.03497 | 0.03497 | 0.0693 |
| Shannon | negative | 0.00488 | 0.00651 | 0.1196 |
| evenness | negative | 0.000507 | 0.00101 | 0.1756 |
| dominance | positive | 0.000367 | 0.00101 | 0.1833 |

Thus Study 2 strongly supports the weaker statement that baseline ecological state relates to subsequent perturbability, while rejecting the stronger statement that the Study 1 direction is universal.

### Genus-level predictive generalization

Genus CLR/PCA composition did not generalize across dairies. For genus mean baseline displacement:

- null RMSE: 0.14572
- genus CLR/PCA RMSE: 0.15394
- RMSE change versus null: -5.65%
- CV R²: -0.219

Simple alpha ecology showed modest but inconsistent transportability:

- null RMSE: 0.14572
- alpha-ecology RMSE: 0.13881
- improvement versus null: 4.74%
- pooled CV R²: 0.009

For Baseline → 7 Days, alpha ecology improved RMSE by 6.97% versus null but pooled CV R² remained slightly negative. For the late target it was 3.95% worse than null.

Interpretation: broad ecological organization is somewhat more transportable than exact taxonomic composition, but predictive portability remains weak and dairy-dependent.

---

## Targeted low-SCC untreated sensitivity analysis

Because Study 2 treatment assignment is entangled with baseline SCC state, a targeted confounding check restricted Q3 to the untreated low-SCC control group only.

At 3k this group contains 14 complete trajectories:

- Dairy 1: 4
- Dairy 2: 3
- Dairy 3: 7

The reverse Study 2 direction persisted in this subgroup.

For ASV mean baseline displacement:

- evenness: beta = -1.157, P = 0.033, incremental R² = 0.325
- dominance: beta = +0.967, P = 0.057, incremental R² = 0.272

For genus mean baseline displacement all four feature directions remained:

- richness: negative
- Shannon: negative
- evenness: negative
- dominance: positive

For genus Baseline → 7 Days, richness and Shannon were negative with nominal P values of 0.0255 and 0.0447, respectively.

Because n = 14, effect direction and magnitude are emphasized rather than significance.

Interpretation: the Study 2 reversal does not disappear when analysis is restricted to untreated low-SCC cows. Antibiotic exposure and high-SCC status alone are therefore insufficient explanations for the Study 1 versus Study 2 directional difference.

---

## Study 1 versus Study 2: what differs biologically

The studies share an important baseline feature: both begin with milk sampled at dry-off before intervention.

However, their longitudinal intervals differ substantially:

### Study 1

`dry-off baseline → calving → 5 DIM`

### Study 2

`dry-off baseline → 7 days after dry-off → 55–75 DIM`

Therefore Study 1 and Study 2 do not expose the microbiome to the same temporal/physiological transition between corresponding numbered timepoints.

Additional cross-study differences include:

- Study 1 follows explicit quarter-level intervention states within each cow.
- Study 2 contains multiple dairies and SCC/treatment strata.
- Exact anatomical-quarter continuity in Study 2 still requires direct verification.
- Sequencing protocols/amplicon regions differ.
- Baseline community taxonomic composition may differ fundamentally between cohorts.

These differences are candidate explanations for heterogeneous Q3 behavior and must be tested rather than assumed.

---

## Current Q3 conclusion

### Strong hypothesis tested

`A universal baseline alpha-diversity rule predicts mammary microbiome perturbability with the same direction across cohorts.`

Current result: **not supported**.

Study 1 showed:

`+ richness, + Shannon, + evenness, - dominance`

Study 2 showed:

`- richness, - Shannon, - evenness, + dominance`

### Weaker hypothesis supported

`Baseline ecological state contains information about subsequent perturbability.`

Current result: **supported within both studies, but with cohort-dependent direction and weak cross-dairy portability in Study 2**.

The combined evidence therefore favors a context-dependent formulation:

`Perturbability = f(Baseline ecological structure, community identity, perturbation regime, ecological background)`

This is a refined hypothesis, not permission to continue unrestricted model searching in Study 2.

---

## Next hypothesis-driven question

The next cross-study question is whether Study 1 and Study 2 begin in fundamentally different baseline taxonomic regimes.

Formal question:

`Are the baseline mammary communities in Study 1 and Study 2 compositionally equivalent ecological backgrounds?`

This is motivated directly by the opposite Q3 directions. Alpha diversity describes community shape but not organism identity; identical Shannon/evenness values can correspond to biologically different communities.

The next analysis should therefore compare baseline communities between the two cohorts at a harmonized taxonomic level, preferably genus level because exact ASVs are not directly portable across different amplicon protocols.

Planned components:

1. shared versus study-specific genera;
2. abundance-weighted shared-genus coverage;
3. baseline genus-level compositional separation and effect size by study;
4. dominant/core genus overlap;
5. overlap of baseline richness/Shannon/evenness/dominance support;
6. if justified, a harmonized shared-genus representation to ask whether the Q3 directional difference persists when community identity is made more comparable.

This analysis tests a mechanistic explanation for failed directional replication. It should remain tightly linked to that question rather than become unrestricted taxa hunting.

---

## Analysis stopping rule

Study 2 is not a feature-discovery cohort. No further machine-learning complexity should be added solely to improve performance.

Avoid unrestricted:

- Random Forest optimization
- gradient boosting optimization
- neural-network modeling
- taxa-by-taxa feature hunting
- post-hoc predictor selection

unless explicitly labeled exploratory and separated from confirmatory conclusions.

The next independent cohort should prospectively test the refined context-modification hypothesis.

# Frozen primary healthy-quarter analysis — 2026-08-16

The current primary MMER analysis is restricted to two phenotype-comparable cohorts:

- Italy: 20 mammary-quarter trajectories from 5 cows
- Manitoba: 29 mammary-quarter trajectories from 9 cows
- Total: 49 quarter trajectories from 14 cows

Wisconsin is excluded from primary inference because individual quarter-level health status could not be reconstructed with sufficient confidence.

## Primary outcome

Ecological resistance is defined as:

R = 1 - Bray-Curtis(T1, T2)

Higher values indicate greater compositional resistance across the dry-off to early-postpartum transition.

## Aim 1

Question:

Does baseline multivariate ecological architecture relate to subsequent ecological resistance?

Model:

R ~ PC1 + PC2 + Study

Cow-clustered CR2 inference was used.

Frozen result:

- joint PC1 + PC2 test: F(2, 7.81) = 10.0
- P = 0.00699
- PC1 beta = -0.0286, P = 0.124
- PC2 beta = +0.0622, P = 0.00302
- adjusted R2 = 0.289

Interpretation:

Baseline mammary community architecture is associated with subsequent ecological resistance, with the pooled association driven predominantly by PC2.

## Aim 2

Question:

Does the architecture-resistance relationship differ between Italy and Manitoba?

Model:

R ~ (PC1 + PC2) * Study

Frozen result:

- interaction test: F(2, 5.55) = 4.23
- P = 0.0766

Interpretation:

There is insufficient conventional evidence of cohort heterogeneity, although effect magnitude may be context dependent.

## PC2 ecological gradient

Mean resistance across PC2 tertiles:

Italy:
- Low: 0.422
- Intermediate: 0.600
- High: 0.656

Manitoba:
- Low: 0.402
- Intermediate: 0.449
- High: 0.492

Positive PC2-loading families include Lactobacillaceae, Pseudomonadaceae, Propionibacteriaceae, Streptococcaceae and Beijerinckiaceae.

Negative PC2-loading families include Paracoccaceae, Bacteroidaceae, Anaerovoracaceae, Ruminococcaceae, Saccharimonadaceae and Caulobacteraceae.

These families define a multivariate ecological state and are not interpreted as individually causal or protective.

## Frozen primary conclusion

Baseline mammary community organization is associated with ecological resistance across the dry-off to early-postpartum transition in healthy mammary quarters.

- scripts/22_primary_healthy_twoAims.R
- scripts/23_PC2_ecological_state_interpretation.R
- scripts/24_publication_figure_primary_healthy.R
